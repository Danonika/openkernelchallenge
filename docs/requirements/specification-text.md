Problem Fine-tuning language models on a single free GPU is limited by memory and time, not by access to the model. Students, researchers and developers without expensive GPUs work on cards such as the Kaggle T4 (about 15 GB, no bf16). Our measurements on a free Kaggle T4 (Soup 0.77.0, Qwen2.5-1.5B, 4-bit, batch 4, seq 512, 256 Kazakh instructions, 64 steps): ● the soup train command takes about 80 seconds (about a minute of it is the training itself, about 900 tokens/s) and uses about 14 of the 15 GB of GPU memory; ● Unsloth trains a similar LoRA on the same data and the same GPU in about 2.3 GB, that is 6 times less; ● the step profile of Soup itself on this task: the GPU is busy about 95% of the time; about 36% goes to matrix multiplication, about 44% to small element-wise operations and dtype casts, about 7% to the softmax over the 152 thousand token vocabulary, about 5% to attention and about 5% to unpacking the 4-bit weights. So there is headroom, but it is unclear how far it can be pushed with your own kernels. That is what we want to find out at the hackathon. Value.
